Instructions for this activity

Efficacy or Effectiveness?

About 8 minutes

The reading separates two kinds of outcome research. An efficacy study tests a treatment under tightly controlled conditions. An effectiveness study asks how clients fare in ordinary practice. Each round draws ten invented study descriptions at random. Sort each one, then read which features of the design point to the answer.

Best done after the reading's section "Does Therapy Work? The Outcome Debate." Remember that efficacy here means treatment efficacy, a property of a treatment, and is different from self-efficacy, a client's belief in their own capability.

This activity scores only the labels you pick, and nothing is saved or sent. There's nothing to write and nothing to submit.

What it adds to your clinical thinking. Reading a study for the question it was built to answer before applying it to the client in front of you.

Efficacy or Effectiveness?

Each description is an invented outcome study. Decide whether it is mainly an efficacy study or an effectiveness study, and see which design features give it away.

The two designs.
Efficacy: random assignment to treatment or a comparison group, a detailed manual, a fixed number of sessions, and participants selected for a single diagnosed disorder.
Effectiveness: ordinary practice, with no fixed length, therapists who change course when something is not working, clients who choose their therapists, clients with more than one problem, and attention to general functioning.
