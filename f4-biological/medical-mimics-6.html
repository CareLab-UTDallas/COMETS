Instructions for this activity

Medical Mimics

About 15 minutes

Five invented clients come to you with what sounds like anxiety, panic, or depression. Each case unfolds one step at a time, from the presenting complaint to the history, a telling detail, and finally an exam or lab finding reported by a physician. At each step you choose what to ask or do next, or which explanations are still on the table. You get immediate feedback with the reason after every choice. The cases appear in a new order each time.

Best done after reading "When the Body Imitates the Mind: Medical Mimics" in the Foundations 4 reading. Your job in every case is the interviewer's job. You ask the questions that make a medical explanation visible, and you refer to a physician. You never diagnose the medical condition yourself.

The activity tracks how many choices you get right on the first try during this session. Nothing is saved or sent, and there is nothing to submit.

What it adds to your clinical thinking. Asking the medical questions that keep a thyroid problem, low blood sugar, or overbreathing from being mistaken for a purely psychological one, and knowing when to refer.

Medical Mimics

Some medical conditions look, sound, and feel like anxiety or depression. Work through each case one step at a time. At every step, decide what to ask, what to do, or what is still possible. The medical question always goes to a physician.

Your role. Ask about medical history, medications, substances, and how the symptoms feel in the body. Notice what does not fit. Refer to a physician and document it. Keep assessing the psychological picture, because a medical cause and a psychological problem can occur together.
