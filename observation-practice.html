Practice behavioral observations with AMSIT

Watch a character, pause often, and write what you observe under each AMSIT area. Then turn those notes into the Behavioral Observations section of a report, the same section that closes the background in the sample for Nate. Only then compare with the expert notes. Describe what you see and hear. Do not diagnose. Observation practice is about noticing and describing accurately, and a fictional character is a safe, endlessly replayable place to build that skill. Any film works, and the fifth option below is for a character of your own choosing.

This page does not save anything. Finish in one sitting and use the copy button to keep your notes. You can come back and practice as often as you like.

Choose a character

Apparent age, grooming, dress, manner of relating to others, psychomotor activity, eye contact, and speech.

Mood as the character would describe it, and the affect you observe, its quality, range, stability, and congruence.

Alertness, orientation, attention, and memory, as shown in what you watch.

An estimate, from vocabulary, knowledge, education, and reasoning.

Thought process and content, any perceptual disturbance, insight, and judgment.

This is the section as it would appear in a report. Write one continuous paragraph, in past tense, describing how the character presented during the scenes you watched. Follow the order AMSIT gives you, without the letters as headings. Describe only what you observed or heard said, and close with a sentence on what your observations were based on.

Write something under every area first, since committing to your own observations is the point.
