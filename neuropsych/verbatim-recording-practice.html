Record Responses Word for Word

Listen to an invented examinee answer once, type exactly what was said, and see each word you dropped, changed, or added. A second exercise asks which notation belongs on the form for a short exchange. The questions and stories here are everyday conversation, not test items.

Nothing here is saved. Every person, question, and story is invented. Practice as often as you like.
Speed

An examinee's response is heard once, so each response plays once by default. Beginners may allow one replay.

The recording rules used here
  • Where the manual says to record verbatim, write the examinee's words exactly as spoken, including the ones you think are wrong. Scoring happens against those words, and a supervisor can re-score only what you wrote (earlier edition, p. 42).
  • Write verbal responses verbatim even when they are correct, and note queries, prompts, repetitions, and nonverbal responses (intelligence-scale manual, p. 48).
  • Record during the session. The NACC instructions say never to depend on memory to record responses after the session is over.
  • Self-corrections receive credit. Score the response the examinee intends (earlier edition, p. 46). If a later response is meant to replace an earlier one, score only the last (memory-scale manual, p. 36).
  • For story recall, record the response verbatim and score it after the session (memory-scale manual, pp. 35, 65). The stories are never repeated during recall (p. 35).
  • Slang, informal language, regional pronunciation, and mispronunciation are not penalized when context shows the response is correct, and the criteria list acceptable variations (memory-scale manual, p. 36). That judgment is made at scoring, from the words you wrote.

Scoring in this exercise ignores case, punctuation, and hesitation sounds such as um and uh. Words that carry meaning, such as no, wait, I mean, or maybe, count.

Rules are paraphrased from the sources cited on the Canvas page. Use the manual during a real administration. The earlier edition and intelligence-scale manual are the two intelligence scale manuals cited there, and the memory-scale manual is the memory scale manual cited there.
