Score a paced serial addition record

A simulated examinee hears an invented series of single digits and says a sum after each one. Mark every response the way the record form is marked, mark the dyads, then enter the trial numbers. Every mark and number is checked against the timing and scoring rules, with the reason for each difference.

Nothing here is saved. Every series is invented and excludes the digit 7. None of the course series appears here, and T scores are not computed.

Scoring rules used here
  • The examinee adds the digit just heard to the digit heard before it, never to the sum just spoken. After 8, 2, and 5 the sums are 10 and 7. Saying 15 after the 5 adds it to the sum just spoken.
  • A full series has 50 digits, none of them a 7. The first digit has nothing before it, so a series allows 49 sums. The four series run at 3.0, 2.4, 2.0, and 1.6 seconds per digit.
  • An answer is correct when it is the sum of the two most recent digits and is given before the next digit sounds. A self-correction made before the next digit sounds is credited. One made after it is not.
  • On the form, a correct response gets a check mark (✓). An incorrect or late response is written beside the printed sum (✗ here). An item with no response gets a line through the printed sum (Miss here).
  • Late answers follow the program's rule. A correct sum given after the next digit sounds is written beside the printed sum with no check mark. It counts as attempted but is not credited.
  • Trial score is the number correct, out of 49. The total of the four trials is out of 196.
  • Percentage correct of attempted and percentage attempted (attempted divided by items possible) are written on the form as a description. They are not normed.
  • A dyad is a correct response immediately preceded by a correct response. The first response of a series cannot be a dyad.

Mark each item. ✓ correct and credited. ✗ a response that is not credited. Miss for no response. D marks a dyad. Times are seconds from the first digit.

Trial numbers
