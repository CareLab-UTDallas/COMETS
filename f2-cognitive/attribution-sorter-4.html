Instructions for this activity

Sort the Explanation

About 10 minutes

The reading describes explanatory style, the habitual way a person explains why things happen. Each round draws eight invented client statements at random. For each one, rate the client's explanation on all three dimensions, internal or external, stable or unstable, and global or specific, then check your ratings. You get feedback on each dimension with the reason, so you can see exactly which part of the explanation you read correctly.

Best done after the reading's section on cognitive models of specific problems. Rate the explanation the client gives, not the one you would give and not the true cause of the event. A few statements are about good events, where the pessimistic pattern runs the other way.

This activity scores only the ratings you pick, and nothing is saved or sent. There's nothing to write and nothing to submit.

What it adds to your clinical thinking. Hearing explanatory style in the way a client explains a setback during the interview.

Sort the Explanation

People explain the same kind of setback in very different ways, and the explanation shapes how long and how widely the setback weighs on them. Rate each client's explanation on three dimensions and see the reasoning for each.

The three dimensions.
Internal or external: is the cause about the person, or about the situation and other people?
Stable or unstable: will the cause last, or will it pass?
Global or specific: does the cause reach into many areas of life, or only this one?
