Instructions for this activity

Build the Chain

About 12 minutes

The reading's section "Reading the Function" introduces the antecedent-behavior-consequence (ABC) chain and the four common functions of behavior: attention, escape, tangible, and automatic. This activity walks you through four invented cases, drawn at random from six. For each case you'll do three things. First, sort the pieces of the case into antecedent, behavior, immediate consequence, and delayed consequence, then check your chain. Second, name the function the behavior serves. Third, reveal how Ellis's ABC (activating event, belief, consequence) would read the same case, so you can see why the two models share letters but not meanings.

Every answer comes with a reason. If a piece goes in the wrong place, you can move it and check again. The tracker counts only your first attempt, so it tells you what you can already see without help.

Nothing is saved or sent. There's nothing to write and nothing to submit.

What it adds to your clinical thinking. Hearing what a behavior gets a person, or gets them out of, in an ordinary story.

Build the Chain

Sort each case into an antecedent-behavior-consequence chain, name what keeps the behavior going, and then compare it with Ellis's ABC for the same moment.

Step 1. Build the chain

Assign each piece to a link in the chain. Immediate consequences happen right after the behavior. Delayed consequences build up over days or weeks.

Step 2. Name the function

What does the behavior reliably get this person, or get them out of?

Step 3. The same case, through Ellis's ABC

Both models use the letters A, B, and C. Reveal Ellis's version to see what changes.
