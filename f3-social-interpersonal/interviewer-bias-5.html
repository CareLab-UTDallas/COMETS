Instructions for this activity

Spot the Interviewer's Bias

About 10 minutes

The reading's section on the interviewer's mind names six ordinary biases that follow clinicians into the intake room: the fundamental attribution error, the actor-observer effect, the availability heuristic, the representativeness heuristic, just-world thinking, and the primacy effect. Each round shows you eight invented interview excerpts drawn at random from a larger bank. Each excerpt ends with a note or inference an interviewer wrote down. Your job is to name the bias the note shows. If your first pick is off, you'll see why and can try again. When you land on the right one, you'll see the reason and a corrective move the interviewer could have made.

Best done after reading "The Interviewer's Mind: Biases in Clinical Judgment." The tracker counts only your first pick on each excerpt, so the number tells you what you recognize on sight. Every client and note here is invented.

Nothing is saved or sent. There's nothing to write and nothing to submit.

What it adds to your clinical thinking. Catching your own inferences before they harden into a formulation.
